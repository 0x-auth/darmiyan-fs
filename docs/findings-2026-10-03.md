## 1. Lambda ~ alpha^-6 is real, and my first refutation of it was wrong

I tested the claim by comparing `alpha^-6 = 6.6e12` against Lambda in Planck
units (`2.89e-122`) and reported a failure of 134 orders of magnitude.
**That test was meaningless.** "Proportional" asserts nothing about
magnitude until the prefactor is named, and I had silently set it to 1.

The relation (Beck 2004; Wei, Zou, Li & Xue, arXiv:1605.04571):

```
Lambda = G^2 m_e^6 / (hbar^4 alpha^6)

           predicted   1.362852e-52 m^-2
            observed   1.105600e-52 m^-2
               ratio   1.232681
```

23% on a quantity whose scale is 10^-122 and whose standard discrepancy is
10^120. Dimensions check with no fudge: `G^2 m_e^6 / hbar^4` is m^-2 exactly.

### Three equivalent forms

| normalisation | statement |
|---|---|
| Planck | `Lambda * l_pl^2 = ( m_e / (alpha * m_pl) )^6` |
| electron | `Lambda * r_e^2 = ( l_pl / r_e )^4` |
| as a radius | `L_dS / l_pl = ( r_e / l_pl )^3` |

The single dimensionless number has two faces that are the same number:

```
l_pl / r_e              = 5.7355899587e-21
m_e / (alpha * m_pl)    = 5.7355899661e-21
```

### The exponent belongs to the ruler, not the relation

```
normalisation         Lambda * L^2      exponent n in chi^n
Planck length        2.888137e-122                 6.004488
classical electron    8.779335e-82                 4.004488
```

The gap is exactly 2 - the dimension of Lambda. **So "Lambda ~ alpha^-6" is
well-posed only in Planck normalisation.** In electron units the identical
relation reads alpha^-4. And it is 6.0045, not 6; that 0.0045 is the 23% in
exponent form.

### My "57" factored

I reported the working exponent as 56.878 and called it structureless.

```
exponent of observed Lambda_pl     -56.8782
exponent of m_e/m_pl               -10.4726
6 x that, plus 6                   -56.8357
```

6 x 10.473 - 6. The structure was inside the number I called featureless.
**Lesson: factor an ugly exponent before declaring it has no structure.**

### Why "three independent derivations" is weaker than it sounds

The Boehmer-Harko route carries a factor of 48, discarded as "order unity":

```
      treatment of the 48          Lambda      vs observed
   kept (eq 5 as written)    6.541691e-51          59.1687
discarded (eq 6 published)   1.362852e-52           1.2327
```

`48^(1/6) = 1.906` is fairly called order unity. It enters Lambda at the
**sixth power**, where it is 48. A sixth power destroys order-unity hygiene
- drop a 2pi and Lambda moves by 2.5e8. So the **exponent** is triply
derived; the **magnitude** rests on Beck alone, and specifically on his
axiom B3, "simplicity", which is what sets the prefactor to exactly 1.

### Where the 23% lives - traceable, not diffuse

```
Lambda residual                   1.232681
same thing as a length            0.965736
R_p(bare)/r_e from eq (5)         1.035480
```

Same number. The entire 23% is a -3.43% mismatch in the single
identification `R_p ~ r_e`, amplified sixfold. It is **not** an 8pi
convention artifact - the ratio is identical computed from Lambda or from
rho_Lambda, because the 8pi is on both sides.

### What it does to the missing unit

It does not derive Lambda. It **trades** Lambda for `m_e/m_pl` - one
unexplained number for another. But 10^-122 of unexplained smallness becomes
10^-21, and the sixth power does the rest. For the first time in this work
the missing unit has been *moved* rather than merely named.

**Next test, and the shortest path to something that bites:** chase the
-3.43%. If `R_p ~ r_e` can be made exact with a defensible factor, this
becomes a prediction. Second: the relation implies
`rho_Lambda_dot/rho_Lambda = -6 alpha_dot/alpha`, whose anchor is Webb et
al. 1998-2001 - a signal that has *not* held up cleanly against later VLT,
atomic-clock and Oklo bounds. Re-fit against current d(alpha)/alpha limits.

## 2. The moving substrate: a third failure mode

Let the substrate stretch while the walker walks:

```
D -> D - 1 + h * D^(1-p)
```

| p | stretch term | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `h*D`, grows with D | **horizon** at D = 1/h, to the node |
| 1 | `h`, constant | marginal; horizon iff h > 1 |
| > 1 | `h*D^(1-p)`, dilutes | **floor** - never a horizon |

The **floor** is new to this work. Every earlier horizon was a fixed point
of a map: the walker stopped because the relation stopped. Here the relation
never stops and the walker never stops, and there is still a separation that
can never be closed.

```
h*D*^(1-p) = 1   =>   D* = h^(1/(p-1))      for p > 1
stable  <=>  D* > (p-1)/2
relaxation = D*/(p-1)
```

**A slow floor and an unstable floor are indistinguishable if you do not run
long enough.** A floor with |f'| = 0.9999 read as "wanders" at 6e4 ticks and
settles exactly by 5e5. That is a hazard for every horizon claim in this
work, not just this one, and it is why `relaxation_ticks` is part of the
framework's public result.

The real universe has an event horizon, so the real exponent is **0** -
Lambda is constant. That is the whole content of "dark energy does not
dilute": a substrate that thins out cannot outrun a constant step.

### The exponent is forced

`1/(p-1) = 3` has exactly one solution. So if the floor is to be the *cube*
of the rate - and the cube is what the Lambda relation above hands you -
then **p = 4/3**. With h = r_e/l_pl:

```
floor D* = h^3                5.299876e+60
de Sitter radius / l_pl       5.884250e+60
ratio                             0.900688
```

**This is not evidence.** It is the same identity rewritten; the residual is
the 1.2327 under a square root. Likewise "relaxation ~ 2x the age of the
universe" is forced, since relaxation = 3D* and D* *is* the de Sitter time
in Planck times. One statement, three dresses.

One real consequence: `(p-1)/D* = 6.3e-62`, below float64 resolution against
1. **This floor cannot be simulated at all** - it is algebraic only. Every
other floor in this work was measured.

## 3. The two 4/3s, and why they are not one

The planar Brownian frontier has Hausdorff dimension **exactly 4/3** -
Mandelbrot conjectured 1982, Lawler-Schramm-Werner proved 2000
(math/0010165) via Schramm's SLE. Fields Medals: Werner 2006, Smirnov 2010.

**Why it is the most interesting object here.** The frontier is *defined* as
the set of visited points reachable from infinity without crossing the walk.
That is not a geometric condition - nothing about position decides it. A
point is on the frontier iff **the outside can reach it**. So 4/3 is the
dimension of *reachability-from-outside*: the inside/outside seam this whole
project is built on, with an exact value attached.

| in this project | has a number? |
|---|---|
| inside resolver's proper time tau | yes, 1.7738775833 |
| outside resolver's link count | yes, an integer |
| cost asymmetry between them | yes, 30760x |
| **the boundary between them** | **4/3** |

Every earlier number is a property of one side, or of the machine running
them. 4/3 is a property of the seam - and **it contains no constants**. No
hbar, c, G, alpha, nothing measured. The missing-unit problem does not apply
to it, because there is no unit to miss. The family is exact rationals from
`dim = 1 + kappa/8`: 5/4 LERW, 4/3 SAW, 7/4 percolation and pioneer points,
3/4 cut points, 2 the range.

**But the two 4/3s are not the same.** The Brownian frontier is 4/3 *because
the walk is planar* - in 3D it is about 2.5 and the 4/3 is gone. It is a
theorem about d = 2. Section 2's walk is a scalar separation on a line;
there is no plane in it.

**What would make it one connection instead of two numbers:**

1. a *planar* walk in the construct - two coordinates that both diffuse;
2. the 4/3 must be the box-counted dimension of the construct's own horizon,
   not an exponent in its update rule;
3. **the 4/3 must stop depending on h.** This is the killer. In section 2
   the floor moves as h^3, so everything about it depends on the rate. A
   Hausdorff dimension does not - that is what makes it a dimension.

## 4. Fluctuation as an instrument

Einstein, May 1905 - same year as special relativity, and none of it in the
paper: `<x^2> = 2Dt`, `D = RT/(6 pi eta a N_A)`. The left side is a wobble
you can watch; the right contains Avogadro's number.

```
bead radius      D (m^2/s)     rms in 30 s
   0.212 um     1.0123e-12         7.79 um
   0.520 um     4.1271e-13         4.98 um
```

A microscope and a ruled grid reach that. Perrin inverted it from 1908;
Nobel 1926. **Nothing in the calculation resolves an atom.**

| fluctuation | relation | yields |
|---|---|---|
| Brownian motion (1905) | `<x^2> = 2Dt` | N_A |
| Johnson-Nyquist noise (1928) | `<V^2> = 4kTR df` | k_B |
| shot noise (Schottky 1918) | `<I^2> = 2eI df` | e |

Einstein's move was not "atoms exist". It was **fluctuations carry the
constants**. Noise is not the error bar; noise is where the constant lives.

Brown's own contribution deserves recording precisely: he is remembered for
seeing the jitter, but it counted because he ground up glass, window panes
and old wood and watched those jitter too. The obvious reading was "the
pollen is alive". He killed it himself. **The discovery was the control.**

## 5. FTL, and the sign of the energy

The dividing line is not speed. It is the null energy condition,
`T_uv k^u k^v >= 0`.

| case | exceeds c? | allowed? | what it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| light in vacuum | no | yes | the limit itself |
| cosmic recession | YES | yes | no motion through space |
| phase velocity in a medium | YES | yes | carries no information |
| entanglement correlation | instant | yes | no signal, no causality |
| a ship accelerating | no | NO | E -> infinity as v -> c |
| Alcubierre bubble | YES | MAYBE | **NEC violation** |
| traversable wormhole | YES | MAYBE | **NEC violation** |

Cosmic recession is the one that belongs to this project: two galaxies
separate faster than c and neither moves. That is section 2's horizon
exactly - the substrate outrunning the walker. FTL in that sense is not
hypothetical; it is useless because you cannot ride it.

Negative energy exists: Casimir between plates 1 um apart gives
rho = -4.33e-04 J/m^3, measured since 1948. The door is not locked. But
Ford-Roman bounds how long it can persist - `|rho| * tau^4 <= hbar/c^3`, the
*fourth* power. A lot for almost no time, or almost none for a long time; a
warp drive needs a lot for a long time, which is the one corner the
inequality closes. Pfenning & Ford's figure for a 100 m bubble at 10c is
-6.2e64 kg, about 4e11 times the positive mass of the observable universe.

**Possible because spacetime is not rigid; impossible because steering it
needs the wrong sign of energy at a density and duration hbar forbids. The
limit was never c. The limit is the sign.**
